# Slice 5 — combat feedback and infantry display

Status: design input for GRY-286; engine-agnostic. This document defines presentation behavior only. The simulation remains the sole authority for combat rules and state (ADR 0006).

## Source contract and event inventory

The current combat system does not emit typed combat events. `SimEvent` contains only `(Tick, Subject, Target)`, and no code in `sim/combat/` calls `Emit` (`sim/core/SimWorld.cs`; `tools/sim-host/CONTEXT.md`). The sim-host exposes post-step HP, position, and velocity in unit snapshots, and a unit's disappearance in a later snapshot. It does not serialize `CooldownRemaining`, `CooldownTicks`, or `TargetId`, and it does not provide typed fire, hit, death, or target-transition events (`tools/sim-host/Protocol/Messages.cs:73-80`; `tools/sim-host/HostedWorld.cs:153-180`). These snapshot changes support damage, motion, and disappearance presentation only. Reliable shooter-fire and target cues require a future sim/host data contract. Do not infer a firing unit from HP changes or run a second combat simulation.

`SimWorld` runs at 20 ticks per second: one tick is 50 ms. Tick `T` systems run before the world increments to `T+1`; the host returns the post-step snapshot and captured events for that step. A presentation consumer must read the result before requesting the next step, because the event buffer clears at the start of the next step (`sim/core/SimWorld.cs`; `tools/sim-host/CONTEXT.md`). Combat resolves after navigation and before dead-unit cleanup (`sim/CONTEXT.md`).

| Semantic trigger needed by presentation | Current sim evidence and timing | Visual response | Audio response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fire | Combat applies damage and resets cooldown in the same step (`sim/combat/CombatSystem.cs:35-48`). The sim-host does not serialize cooldown or target fields, so its snapshots cannot identify the shooter or a fire transition. | Desired behavior, conditional on a future reliable fire-data contract: play the infantry `fire` clip and a brief muzzle flash/projectile cue once at the identified firing unit. Use the faction weapon-FX hue, separate from reserved team hues. The host snapshot exposes HP deltas, but no separate hit event, attacker, or warhead. A delta identifies a damaged unit, not the shot that caused it. | A snapshot HP decrease may drive one compact response on that unit using its latest transmitted position. Do not claim attacker or warhead attribution. Distinguish impact families by shape/material, not team-color hues. Keep the silhouette and health state legible. | A snapshot HP decrease may drive a quiet, short damage cue. Rate-limit aggregated or repeated changes; do not claim per-shot audio without event data. |

Target acquisition, target loss, chase-order refresh, and cooldown countdown are combat state changes, not emitted events. The sim-host does not serialize target or cooldown fields, so it cannot drive target-specific lines, fire clips, or one-shot audio. The cited Godot target line reads the in-process `UnitStore`; it is not part of the sim-host contract (`game/render/UnitRenderer.cs:103-108,117-123`). Do not use it as evidence that Unreal receives those fields, and do not add a second combat simulation or render-frame polling.

## Infantry display states

Drive available display state from authoritative snapshots, not from a second combat simulation. The host provides position and velocity for movement and facing. HP changes and unit disappearance can drive damage and a best-effort death response. The host cannot select a shooter `fire` clip or target cue until the fire-data contract exists. The cited Godot renderer reads in-process sim state; it does not expand the host protocol (`docs/asset-pipeline.md:5-11`; `game/render/UnitRenderer.cs:54-61,99-108,188-204`). A presentation-only last transform can support a death clip after disappearance, but it may not equal the unit's final transform for that tick.

| State | Entry condition | Display behavior | Exit / priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idle | Unit is alive and has no meaningful movement velocity; no fire trigger is active. | Play looping `idle`; face the last movement direction. | `move` when velocity becomes non-zero; `fire` when a supported fire trigger occurs; `death` when HP reaches zero or the unit disappears. |
| Move | Unit is alive and snapshot velocity is non-zero. | Play looping `move`; face velocity. Interpolate between completed-tick positions. | `idle` when movement stops; `fire` when a supported fire trigger occurs; `death` when HP reaches zero or the unit disappears. |
| Fire | A reliable fire trigger identifies this unit and tick. The current sim-host cannot provide this trigger; this state is enabled only after the fire-data contract is implemented. | Play the one-shot `fire` clip and muzzle response once for the trigger. Do not change sim position or block subsequent simulation ticks. | Return to `move` or `idle` when the one-shot ends; `death` interrupts immediately. |
| Death | The unit disappears from the next authoritative unit set. The last transmitted transform may precede the actual death step; exact final placement needs a future death-data contract. | Freeze at the last transmitted transform, play one best-effort `death` presentation, then remove the view and effects after their bounded lifetime. Never recreate or control the dead sim unit. | Terminal. Death overrides fire, move, and idle. |

State priority is `death > fire > move > idle`. Missing animation assets must not break simulation or visibility; use a readable static pose/proxy and preserve the same transition rules. Infantry assets should provide `idle`, `move`, `fire`, and `death` clips for this slice; the current asset pipeline names only the first three as its minimum (`docs/asset-pipeline.md:31-35`).

## Readability and direction constraints

Apply the priority `FUN · READABILITY · FACTION IDENTITY · SPECTACLE`; readability wins when spectacle conflicts (`docs/design-direction.md:57-61`; `docs/readability.md:7`). These rules apply to every event response and infantry state:

- Preserve unit/terrain luminance separation in gameplay views, including impact, fire, and death frames. Check the desaturated default-zoom view; an effect must not make the unit merge into the ground (`docs/readability.md:11-25`).
- Keep each role's silhouette distinct. Keep muzzle flashes brief, explosions short, scorch low-contrast, and smoke limited in screen-space density (`docs/readability.md:27-33,53-62`).
- Reserve the two team-identity hues for team identity. Weapon effects may use faction identity hues, but those hues must not reuse the reserved team hues (`docs/readability.md:41-51`). Never use color as the only event or state signal (`docs/readability.md:74-90`).
- Show meaningful state in the world, not only in a selection panel. Keep visible health/damage state legible while units move, fire, and die (`docs/readability.md:53-62`).
- Keep the industrial tone and original faction identity. Use references as principles, not as instructions to copy a recognizable unit or effect (`docs/design-direction.md:22-30,43-55`).
- Make response weight follow player significance, not raw event count. Rate-limit repeated sounds and effects so a dense firefight does not become visual or audio noise (`docs/mechanics-playbook.md:61-65`).

## GRY-286 acceptance checks

Review the actual Unreal slice against these observable checks. A source-code or headless check does not replace visual review in the running scene.

1. Conditional on implementing a reliable fire-data contract that identifies the firing unit and sim tick, trigger a shot. The `fire` clip, muzzle cue, and shot sound each occur once for that trigger. No cue repeats on render-only frames. The current host snapshot cannot pass this check; do not substitute HP or cooldown heuristics.
2. Cause a non-lethal hit. The target's HP delta appears in the post-step snapshot, and one short response may follow that damaged-unit change without obscuring its silhouette or health indicator. Do not claim attacker, warhead, or per-shot attribution.
3. Kill infantry. The next snapshot omits the unit. The view plays at most one best-effort death response at its last transmitted transform, then the view is removed. If exact death-tick placement is required, make this check conditional on a contract carrying the death tick and final transform. The dead unit cannot move, fire, or return to an alive animation.
4. Exercise idle-to-move and move-to-idle using host position/velocity. Exercise move-to-fire, fire-to-move, and fire-to-idle only after the fire-data contract is available. Facing follows velocity, the fire one-shot returns to the correct locomotion state, and death interrupts every other state.
5. Advance ticks at the 20 Hz sim rate. Snapshot-driven presentation follows each returned post-step state; render interpolation does not advance, delay, or alter sim state. Do not infer an effect from an untyped `SimEvent` or unavailable host field.
6. Inspect the full battle at default zoom and a close view, then inspect a desaturated default-zoom frame. Units remain separated from terrain, team identity remains clear, and fire/impact/death effects do not hide units or their health state (`docs/readability.md:11-25,41-62,80-90`).
7. Confirm weapon-FX colors differ from reserved team-identity hues, and that event type remains distinguishable without color alone (`docs/readability.md:41-51,74-90`).
8. Run a dense exchange. Repeated sound and effect cues remain rate-limited and do not obscure who is shooting whom; targeting remains legible at default zoom (`docs/game-design-pillars.md:7-12`; `docs/mechanics-playbook.md:61-65`).

## Current limitation

The current sim provides no typed fire, hit, death, or target-transition events, and `SimEvent` does not include event kind, damage amount/type, or position. The sim-host snapshots expose HP, position, velocity, and unit presence, but omit cooldown and target data. HP deltas, movement, and disappearance are current presentation inputs; they do not identify a firing unit, target transition, weapon, or exact death transform. Combat decrements and resets cooldown in the same step, so consecutive post-step snapshots need not show a zero-to-positive transition (`sim/combat/CombatSystem.cs:23,39-47`). Same-step cleanup also removes a killed unit before the host returns its post-step snapshot. GRY-286 must treat fire- and exact-death-dependent checks as conditional on a future sim/host contract; that contract should carry event kind, tick, relevant entity IDs, and position as needed. Do not add a second combat simulation in Unreal or this documentation-only task.
